THE FASHION
EMAIL PROBLEM

01. DIAGNOSIS

Fashion and activewear email has a well-documented problem. Subscribers sign up during a discount moment (a first-purchase offer, a seasonal sale, a welcome popup), collect the code, and disengage. The brand used a discount to acquire the email address. The subscriber associated the email address with discounts. When the discounts stop, so does the engagement.

Most fashion email programs try to solve this by sending more frequently and leaning harder on promotions. That compounds the problem. Open rates drop as subscribers learn that most emails don't warrant attention. Unsubscribe rates climb when sends feel like noise rather than signal. The spiral accelerates.

The industry average for fashion email open rate is around 20-25%. Click rates average 2-2.5%. Brands that run discount-heavy programs often land below both. The goal with this activewear brand was to build an email program where engagement held above both benchmarks without needing to lean on constant promotional incentives to drive it.

SEGMENTATION
AS STRATEGY

02. CAMPAIGN STRUCTURE

With 62 flows handling the always-on lifecycle work, the 50 campaign sends were reserved for moments that warranted a full broadcast: product drops, seasonal releases, and a limited number of promotional moments tied to real occasions rather than calendar-filling sends.

Each campaign was segmented before it sent. VIP subscribers received early access before the general list. Engaged subscribers received the standard version. Unengaged subscribers (those who hadn't opened in the last 90 days) were excluded entirely, protecting the open rate and the sender domain simultaneously.

Brand voice consistency was enforced across every touchpoint. Fashion email fails when it looks like a different brand each week: inconsistent design, inconsistent tone, inconsistent offer logic. Every send followed the same visual identity and communication style, which is part of why the unsubscribe rate held at 0.349%: subscribers knew what they were getting, and they chose to stay subscribed.

THE SEGMENTATION TIERS
VIP

Early access to drops. Exclusive acknowledgement. Different copy tone. Higher-intent audience treated as such.

ENGAGED

Standard campaign sends. Consistent cadence. Full campaign access on release day.

LAPSING

Win-back flow triggered. Not included in standard campaign sends until re-engagement is confirmed.

UNENGAGED

Excluded from campaigns. Suppressed after win-back flow concludes without re-engagement.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

How do you maintain a 43.3% open rate across 50+ campaigns? [+]

Consistency at that level comes from three disciplines: sending only to engaged segments, writing subject lines that earn the open without relying on discounts, and maintaining a steady send cadence that conditions subscribers to expect and open the email. Fashion audiences are harder to maintain: the novelty factor drops fast if the content doesn't earn attention. The 43.3% average reflects a brand that built an audience that genuinely wanted the content.

Is a 2.86% click rate good for fashion email? [+]

Yes, and more importantly, it's consistent. Industry click rates for fashion typically land between 1.0–2.0%. Sustaining 2.86% across 50 campaigns means the email content was reliably driving action, not just occasional spikes from one-time promotions. That kind of click consistency signals that the copy, creative, and offer were working together across the full campaign set.
