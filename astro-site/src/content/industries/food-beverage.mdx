## The Problem

Specialty food and beverage brands often assume a modest list or a small catalog means email isn't worth building out properly. That's backwards. A focused catalog is easier to build a tight, intentional system around; the mistake is either not building anything, or copying an oversized flow stack meant for a much bigger, broader catalog.

## How We Approach Food & Beverage Email

- **A lean flow stack scoped to your actual catalog**, not a generic 20-flow template built for a brand with ten times the SKU count.
- **Storytelling and sourcing content in campaigns**, since a focused catalog means content strategy leans on brand story and use case more than constant new-product announcements.
- **Replenishment and subscription flows** timed to actual consumption or shelf life, particularly relevant for perishable or subscription-model products.
- **Segmentation by purchase frequency**, which tends to matter more than product category when the catalog itself is small.

## Proof From Food & Beverage Accounts

One specialty food brand with a modest list and no prior email infrastructure got a tight, intentional 15-flow stack: no excess, no filler, generating $18,501 from flows alone. The lesson: a small list built well outperforms a large list with no system behind it.
