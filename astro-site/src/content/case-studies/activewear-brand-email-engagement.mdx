## The Fashion Email Problem

Fashion and activewear email has a well-documented problem. Subscribers
sign up during a discount moment (a first-purchase offer, a seasonal
sale, a welcome popup), collect the code, and disengage. The brand used a
discount to acquire the email address. The subscriber associated the
email address with discounts. When the discounts stop, so does the
engagement.

Most fashion email programs try to solve this by sending more frequently
and leaning harder on promotions. That compounds the problem. Open rates
drop as subscribers learn that most emails don't warrant attention.
Unsubscribe rates climb when sends feel like noise rather than signal.
The spiral accelerates.

The industry average for fashion email open rate is around 20-25%. Click
rates average 2-2.5%. Brands that run discount-heavy programs often land
below both. The goal with this activewear brand was to build an email
program where engagement held above both benchmarks without needing to
lean on constant promotional incentives to drive it.

## The Automation Foundation

58 flows is not a number chosen arbitrarily. Every flow exists because a
specific subscriber moment required a specific automated response. The
program treats email less like a broadcast channel and more like a CRM.

- **Acquisition flows — Welcome & Onboarding:** Welcome sequences, SMS
  opt-in prompts, and early education flows introduced new subscribers to
  the brand before they encountered a campaign. First impressions were
  managed, not left to chance.
- **Behavioral flows — Browse & Abandon:** Browse abandonment, cart
  abandonment, and checkout recovery flows fired on observed behavior.
  These triggered when it mattered, when a subscriber had shown intent,
  not on a generic schedule.
- **Retention flows — Post-Purchase & Loyalty:** Post-purchase sequences,
  VIP early-access flows, and loyalty acknowledgement turned single
  buyers into repeat customers without requiring a new campaign send for
  each interaction.
- **Win-back & sunset — Unengaged profiles handled systematically:**
  Before any profile hit a suppression threshold, a win-back flow gave it
  every opportunity to re-engage. Profiles that didn't respond were
  removed cleanly, protecting sender reputation and keeping the active
  list percentage high. This is the part most fashion brands skip, and
  it's the main reason their deliverability degrades over time.

**Total:** 58 active flows, covering every lifecycle stage, running in
parallel, around the clock.

## Segmentation as Strategy

With 58 flows handling the always-on lifecycle work, the 46 campaign
sends were reserved for moments that warranted a full broadcast: product
drops, seasonal releases, and a limited number of promotional moments
tied to real occasions rather than calendar-filling sends.

Each campaign was segmented before it sent. VIP subscribers received
early access before the general list. Engaged subscribers received the
standard version. Unengaged subscribers (those who hadn't opened in the
last 90 days) were excluded entirely, protecting the open rate and the
sender domain simultaneously.

Brand voice consistency was enforced across every touchpoint. Fashion
email fails when it looks like a different brand each week: inconsistent
design, inconsistent tone, inconsistent offer logic. Every send followed
the same visual identity and communication style, which is part of why
the unsubscribe rate held at 0.32%: subscribers knew what they were
getting, and they chose to stay subscribed.

**The segmentation tiers:**

- **VIP:** Early access to drops. Exclusive acknowledgement. Different
  copy tone. Higher-intent audience treated as such.
- **Engaged:** Standard campaign sends. Consistent cadence. Full campaign
  access on release day.
- **Lapsing:** Win-back flow triggered. Not included in standard campaign
  sends until re-engagement is confirmed.
- **Unengaged:** Excluded from campaigns. Suppressed after win-back flow
  concludes without re-engagement.

## The Delta

Quantitative outcomes from 12 months of Klaviyo campaign and flow
reporting. Most email programs beat the open rate benchmark or the click
rate benchmark. Beating both simultaneously is significantly harder,
especially in fashion, where a subscriber base acquired through discount
incentives tends to click less and unsubscribe more as promotional
frequency increases.

- **Avg click rate:** 2.66% (above industry avg)
