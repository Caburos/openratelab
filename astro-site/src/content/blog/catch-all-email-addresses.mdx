Catch-all email addresses are the ones a verification tool cannot confirm, so it labels them risky or unknown. Deleting all of them is the quickest way to tidy a list, and it usually removes real subscribers along with the doubtful ones. A better move is to sort them by what each contact has actually done.

A catch-all domain, sometimes called accept-all, is set up so the mail server accepts mail for any address at that domain, whether or not a mailbox exists. Verification tools work by asking the receiving server whether an address exists. A catch-all server answers yes to everything, so the tool cannot tell a working mailbox from a made-up one and falls back on a vague label.

## What Catch-All Email Addresses Are And Why Tools Call Them Risky

A catch-all address sits on a domain whose server accepts every message, so no outside tool can prove the specific mailbox is real. The label you see depends on the vendor. Risky, unknown and accept-all all describe the same situation: the tool could not decide.

That uncertainty is the whole finding. Many of these addresses belong to real people, such as company addresses, small businesses and personal domains. On a consumer-focused list they are usually a minority, and the share rises where you sell to businesses or collect work emails.

Because the tool is guessing, a risky label tells you where to look, and it does not tell you what to do.

## Why Deleting Every Risky Address Costs You Real Subscribers

A blanket delete treats uncertainty as guilt. The risky group mixes engaged customers with a few addresses that are genuinely dead, and removing all of them shrinks your list and your revenue in return for a small, unmeasured gain in quality.

There is also a signal you may be ignoring. These addresses have not bounced. If a large share of them were dead, your bounce report would already show it. Our guide to [why a bounce rate keeps climbing after a list clean](/blog/email-bounce-rate-keeps-climbing) covers what to do when bounces do appear. Here the question is different: what do you do with contacts that are merely unverified?

One caveat applies. Some catch-all servers accept a message and then reject or discard it afterward, so bounce reporting for these contacts can lag or look different from a normal hard bounce. That is a reason to measure the group carefully, and it gives no grounds for deleting the group unseen.

## Sort Risky Addresses By What They Have Done

Behaviour is better evidence than a verification label, so split the risky group by purchase and engagement history. Four buckets cover most lists:

1. **Placed an order.** Keep them. A purchase is the strongest proof that a real person reads that mailbox.
2. **Clicked recently.** Keep them, but weigh clicks with care. Company mail systems sometimes scan links automatically, which can record clicks nobody made, so purchases are the safer signal.
3. **Joined long ago and never opened or clicked.** Suppress them. They carry risk and bring no revenue. The tiers and win-back steps in our guide to [email list hygiene in Klaviyo](/blog/email-list-hygiene-klaviyo) apply here unchanged.
4. **Joined recently and flagged risky.** Hold them for confirmation. A welcome email that asks for a click, or a double opt-in form, proves the mailbox works before the full flow starts.

The goal is to remove the contacts that add only risk and keep the ones that add revenue, using your own data instead of a vendor's guess.

## Test The Keepers Before You Trust Them

Send one normal campaign to the risky contacts you decided to keep, and read its bounce and complaint rates separately from the rest of the list. A small, clean test settles the question faster than any amount of debate about the label.

If you verify addresses with an outside tool, import the result into Klaviyo as a custom profile property so you can build a segment on it. Property and segment labels vary by version, so check your own account for the exact names.

Compare that segment with your overall numbers after the send:

- Bounce rate close to the rest of the list: the label was cautious, and these contacts can stay in your normal sends.
- Bounce rate clearly higher: tighten the rules, suppress the weakest bucket and repeat the test.
- Complaints higher than elsewhere: reduce the frequency to this group before you touch anything else.

Repeat the check after the next one or two sends, because a late bounce from an accept-all server may only show up then.

## Catch Risky Addresses At The Door Next Time

The cheapest fix is to stop collecting unconfirmed addresses in volume. Verify at signup where your form supports it, and consider a confirmation step for any address a tool flags. That keeps the risky group small, so each future decision involves a handful of contacts instead of a few thousand.
