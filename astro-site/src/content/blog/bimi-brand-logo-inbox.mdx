A verified logo next to your sender name sounds like a quick visual win. In practice, it's the last step in an authentication setup that has to already be working, not something you can add on top of a setup that isn't.

## What BIMI Actually Does

BIMI displays a verified brand logo next to the sender name in supporting inboxes. That's the entire function. It's a trust signal for the person looking at their inbox, not a deliverability mechanism.

It doesn't help a message get past spam filtering and doesn't improve inbox placement on its own. A message still has to pass the same authentication and reputation checks it would without BIMI. The logo only appears once all of that has already succeeded.

## Why DMARC Has To Already Be Enforcing

BIMI's specification requires a DMARC policy of p=quarantine or p=reject. A domain still sitting at p=none, which is the starting policy for most setups, doesn't qualify.

The reasoning is straightforward: BIMI is a visual trust signal layered on top of authentication that's actually protecting the domain. A domain at p=none hasn't proven anything yet, since a spoofed message using that domain wouldn't be blocked or flagged. Showing a logo for a domain that could still be impersonated without consequence would make the logo meaningless as a trust signal.

That dependency makes BIMI the step that comes after DMARC enforcement has been live and stable, not a parallel project you can run at the same time.

## The Certificate Requirement Most Guides Skip Past

Gmail and Yahoo require a Verified Mark Certificate, a VMC, before they'll display the logo. The certificate validates that the logo belongs to a registered trademark, and it's issued by a small number of authorized certificate authorities, not something a domain can self-assert its way into.

That requirement means BIMI for the major inboxes depends on having a registered trademark in the first place, which isn't true for every brand. Apple Mail is the one notable exception, supporting a self-asserted logo record without a VMC, but it's the exception, not something to plan the whole rollout around.

## The Logo Format Requirements That Trip People Up

BIMI requires the logo in SVG Tiny 1.2 PS format, a constrained SVG profile, not a regular SVG export from a design tool. A standard SVG export usually fails validation, and the fix is converting it through a tool built specifically for this profile rather than troubleshooting the export settings in whatever generated it.

This is a small technical detail, but it's the kind of thing that stalls a BIMI rollout for a week if nobody on the team has hit it before.

## Do This Only Once Authentication Is Already Stable

BIMI is worth pursuing once DMARC enforcement has been live long enough to be genuinely stable, not as a parallel project alongside getting authentication right. If [your DMARC setup still has gaps at the seam between your email platform and your DNS host](/blog/dmarc-multi-vendor-dns), that's the thing to fix first. A verified logo on top of an authentication setup that isn't solid yet just adds another moving part to a foundation that still needs work.
