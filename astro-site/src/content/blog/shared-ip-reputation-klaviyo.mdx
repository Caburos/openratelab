Shared IP reputation is the deliverability variable most Klaviyo accounts never think to check, because most accounts don't know they're on a shared IP at all. Klaviyo, like most major ESPs, sends the majority of accounts through shared IP pools by default.

That matters because email reputation attaches to the sending IP address itself, not just to your domain. On a shared pool, that means other senders using the same IP affect your deliverability, whether or not your own sending is clean.

## What A Shared IP Pool Actually Means For Your Account

A shared IP pool is a block of sending IP addresses that Klaviyo (or any ESP) routes multiple customer accounts through. The default assignment exists for a real reason: a low-volume sender alone usually can't generate enough consistent sending activity to build a strong independent reputation, while a well-managed pool with steady aggregate volume can maintain one more reliably.

Dedicated IPs exist, but they're an upgrade path, not the default. They make sense once an account's own volume is high enough to maintain reputation on its own, not before.

## How Reputation Attaches To The IP, Not Just The Domain

Inbox providers like Gmail and Outlook track sender reputation at the IP level as one of several signals, alongside domain-level signals like SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment. A clean domain reputation doesn't fully insulate you from a damaged IP reputation, because the two are tracked separately and both feed into the final placement decision.

On a shared pool, that IP-level signal reflects every sender using it, not just you. If another account on the same pool has poor list hygiene, high complaint rates, or gets flagged for spam-like sending, that drags down the pool's reputation, and your mail rides on the same reputation even though your own sending never changed.

This is why two accounts with identical list quality, identical authentication, and identical content can see different deliverability results if they're on different shared pools. The variable isn't anything either account did. It's which pool each one happens to be assigned to at that moment.

This is the part most deliverability advice misses, because most advice assumes the only variables are the ones you directly control. [Our deliverability diagnostic framework](/blog/deliverability-collapse-order) covers the sequence most collapses actually follow, and a shared pool issue specifically sits at the authentication and infrastructure stage, the one most teams check last, not first.

## How To Check If A Shared Pool Problem Is Actually Yours

Audit your own sending first. List hygiene, engagement rates, complaint rates, and your SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment all need to check out clean before looking anywhere else. Our guide to [SPF, DKIM, and sender reputation for DTC brands](/blog/email-deliverability-guide-dtc) covers what a genuinely clean setup actually looks like.

If your own numbers are clean and deliverability is still dropping, that's the point to ask your ESP's support or deliverability team directly whether the shared pool itself has had recent reputation issues. Most ESPs can check this on their end even when it isn't visible from your own account dashboard.

The distinction matters because the fix is completely different depending on the answer. A problem in your own sending gets fixed by cleaning your list or tightening your content. A problem in the shared pool doesn't respond to any of that, because the issue isn't something your own account controls.

## When A Dedicated IP Is Actually Worth It

Volume is the real gate here, not preference. A dedicated IP needs enough consistent daily sending to build and maintain its own reputation on its own, generally in the tens of thousands of emails per month range as a rough floor. Below that, a dedicated IP usually performs worse than a well-managed shared pool, because there isn't enough volume to establish a strong independent signal.

If your volume clears that bar and a shared pool keeps causing recurring problems, a dedicated IP trades shared-pool risk for full control over your own sending reputation. That's a real tradeoff worth making at the right volume, not a default upgrade worth chasing below it.

## What To Do If The Shared Pool Is Genuinely The Problem

If your own sending checks out clean and your ESP confirms a pool-level issue, the options are limited but real: wait for the pool's reputation to recover with the provider's own remediation (most ESPs actively monitor and manage this), ask about moving to a different shared pool if one's available, or evaluate whether your volume actually supports a dedicated IP as described above.

What doesn't help is treating a pool-level problem like a content or segmentation problem. Rewriting subject lines or tightening targeting won't move a reputation issue that isn't coming from your own account in the first place.

It's also worth revisiting this check periodically, not just once. Pool assignments and pool-level reputation aren't static: an ESP can rebalance which accounts share which infrastructure over time, so a clean pool today isn't a permanent guarantee, the same way a dirty one today isn't necessarily permanent either.
