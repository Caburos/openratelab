Klaviyo A/B testing works when the test answers one question with enough data behind it, and most tests fail on both counts. A winner declared after a few hours on a small send tells you very little about what your list prefers.

The setup below is what we use to make a result worth acting on: one variable, a hypothesis written first, a metric that matches the question, and a fixed end date.

## Why Most Klaviyo A/B Testing Proves Nothing

Most tests prove nothing because the result is read too early or the two versions differ in several ways at once. A subject line test that also changes the sender name and the send time cannot say which change did anything.

The second cause is volume. Small differences between two versions show up as noise on a small audience, so a gap of a few points between version A and version B can appear and then vanish on the next send. Klaviyo's testing tools can flag results that are not statistically significant, though the exact wording and options change between versions of the interface, so check what your account shows before you trust a winner.

## Change One Variable And Write The Hypothesis First

Test one variable per send and write the hypothesis before you build the test. A usable hypothesis names the change and the expected effect, for example: a subject line that leads with the product name will get more clicks than one that leads with the offer.

Writing it down first stops a common bad habit, which is looking at the results and then inventing a reason for them. It also keeps your list of things to test short. Subject line, preview text, sender name, the main image, the wording of the call to action and send time are the usual candidates, and each one deserves its own send.

## Pick A Metric That Matches The Question

Choose the metric before the send and match it to what the change could actually influence. A subject line can move opens, but opens have been unreliable since Apple Mail Privacy Protection started loading emails automatically, a problem we cover in our post on [whether you can trust your open rate data](/blog/open-rate-data-accuracy). A winner picked on opens alone can be a winner on inflated numbers.

For subject line and preview text tests, clicks are the safer measure, because a click needs a person. For content, layout and call to action tests, use click rate or, where you have enough orders to read it, placed order rate or revenue per recipient. If a test lifts opens while clicks stay flat, the pattern we describe in [high open rate, low click rate](/blog/high-open-rate-low-click-rate) is worth reading before you roll the result out.

## Sample Size And Duration: Wait For Enough Data

Set the audience size and the end date before the send, and do not stop early because one version looks ahead. There is no universal minimum list size we can give you, because the number you need depends on how large a difference you want to detect and on your usual click rate. A big lift is visible on a smaller audience, and a small lift needs a much larger one.

Duration matters as much as volume. In general, opens and clicks cluster in the first hours after a send and orders often trail in over the following days, so a result read after a couple of hours reflects the fastest responders only. If you use a feature that sends the winner to the rest of the list automatically, give the test period enough room to cover slower readers, because that window is what the winner is chosen from.

## When Your List Is Too Small To Test

When the audience is small, test bigger changes or repeat the same idea across several sends. A list that cannot produce a significant result on a subject line can still learn from a large swing, such as a completely different offer or call to action, because large differences show up even in smaller samples.

The other option is repetition. If the same version wins across three or four campaigns, that consistency carries more weight than one significant-looking result. Keep a simple record so you can see the pattern, and treat a single small-list win as a lead to retest.

## What To Do With A Winner

Log every test and use the winner as the starting hypothesis for the next one. A short record with the date, the variable, the two versions, the metric, the result and the audience builds a library of what your subscribers respond to, and it stops you from rerunning a test you already ran last spring.

Then apply the result where it fits. A subject line pattern that works in campaigns can carry over to flows such as the [welcome series](/blog/klaviyo-welcome-series-guide), though each flow deserves its own check because a new subscriber and a long-time customer respond to different things.
