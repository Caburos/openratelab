To reactivate a dormant Klaviyo account safely, restart in stages instead of sending to the whole list at once. A quiet account carries an old list and a reputation that mailbox providers have had months to forget, so the first sends decide how the account is treated from here on.

The order we use is simple. Audit what is still connected, clean the list, send to the most engaged contacts first, and rebuild your flows before you schedule any campaigns.

## Why a Quiet Account Is Not a Clean Slate

A paused account keeps its list but loses its momentum. The contacts are still there, yet some of their addresses have changed or been abandoned, and the engagement history that signaled a healthy sender has gone stale.

When sending resumes, mailbox providers see a sender that was silent and is suddenly sending a large volume. That pattern can look like a list that has not been maintained, and the early results (bounces, complaints, low engagement) can set the tone for later sends. This is why the size of the first send matters less than who receives it.

## Step 1: Audit What Is Still Connected and Running

Start by checking what the account is actually doing today. Accounts that go quiet often keep running something in the background, and it is better to find that before you add more sends on top.

Work through this list:

- **Integrations.** Confirm your store platform is still syncing orders, customers and events. A broken connection means flows and segments are working from incomplete data.
- **Live flows.** Open each flow and check its status. Old flows may still be live, may point to expired offers, or may reference products you no longer sell.
- **Sending domain and authentication.** Confirm your domain is still set up correctly and that the authentication records are still in place. Our [deliverability guide for DTC brands](/blog/email-deliverability-guide-dtc) explains what to check.
- **Signup forms.** Check that forms still work and that the consent language is current.

Fix what is broken before you send anything. Restarting on top of a broken sync produces confusing results that are hard to diagnose later.

## Step 2: Clean the List Before the First Send

Clean the list first, because the contacts you send to in the first weeks matter more than the ones you send to later. Look at who is still safe to mail.

Suppress addresses that have already bounced. Then build a segment of contacts with no opens or clicks across a long window, using a period that fits how often you used to send. Hold that group back from the first sends instead of deleting it outright. Our guide to [email list hygiene in Klaviyo](/blog/email-list-hygiene-klaviyo) shows one way to tier those contacts.

Keep recent buyers and recent subscribers in the active pool even if they have been quiet, since they have the strongest reason to recognize your name. How you draw these lines depends on your own data, so check your numbers rather than copying a window from another account.

## Step 3: Ramp Up With the Most Engaged Contacts First

Begin with the people most likely to open and click, and widen the audience in steps. Engaged contacts give mailbox providers the positive signals that a returning sender needs.

A practical sequence looks like this:

1. Send a small, relevant message to your most engaged segment.
2. Watch bounce rate, spam complaints and engagement before doing anything else.
3. If results look healthy, add the next tier of contacts to the following send.
4. Pause the expansion if results worsen, and work out why before you continue.

There is no universal volume schedule that fits every account. Your own numbers decide the pace, so use them as the brake. Going slower than you need costs a few days. Going faster than the account can take can cost weeks of recovery.

## Step 4: Rebuild Flows Before Campaigns

Flows earn revenue without depending on a big send, which makes them the safest place to restart. A welcome flow, an abandoned cart flow and a post-purchase flow reach people at moments of clear intent, so they tend to generate positive engagement signals on their own.

Put these in place before you schedule a promotional campaign. Once flows are running and your engagement numbers look steady, campaigns can return to the calendar.

The contacts you held back in Step 2 are a separate job. A win-back flow gives them one clear chance to re-engage, and anyone who still stays silent can be suppressed without guilt.

## What to Watch in the First Weeks

Track a small set of signals and compare them against your own earlier numbers: bounce rate, spam complaint rate, and engagement among the contacts you actually sent to. Keep in mind that open rates have their own accuracy limits, so weigh clicks and conversions alongside them.

If one of those signals moves the wrong way, slow down and narrow the audience again. A restart is a process you adjust as results come in, and the goal is to arrive at a list and a sender reputation you can trust.
