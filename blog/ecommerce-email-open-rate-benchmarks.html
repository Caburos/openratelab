BENCHMARKS / DATA — 6 MIN READ — 2026-07-18

ECOMMERCE EMAIL OPEN RATE BENCHMARKS:
REAL DATA FROM ACTIVE KLAVIYO ACCOUNTS.

Industry benchmark reports average data across thousands of accounts, including brands with broken setups. We pulled numbers from accounts we actually manage to give you a cleaner reference point.

When someone asks "what's a good open rate for email marketing?", the honest answer is: it depends on what you're sending, to whom, and how clean your list is. Industry averages are useful context — they're not a performance target.

The benchmarks published by email platforms aggregate data across every account on their platform, including brands that haven't touched their flows in two years, haven't suppressed a single unengaged subscriber, and send the same promotional blast to everyone regardless of purchase history. That drags the average down considerably.

What follows are benchmarks from the accounts we manage — brands that have cleaned lists, built segmentation, and have active flow architectures. These are the numbers you're competing against when you're competing against best-in-class, not average.

The Industry Benchmark Baseline

First, the baseline. Klaviyo's published benchmarks for ecommerce put the average campaign open rate at approximately 35–40% post-iOS 15 (when Apple's Mail Privacy Protection inflated open rates for brands relying on pixel tracking). For brands still measuring opens without accounting for MPP inflation, real open rates are often 10–15 points lower than reported.

Klaviyo's own open rate data reflects machine opens from Apple devices as "opened," which means many accounts report higher open rates than they actually earn through genuine subscriber attention. When benchmarking, always compare against accounts using the same measurement methodology.

Why These Numbers Are Higher Than Average

It's worth being specific about what drives the gap between industry average and what well-run accounts actually achieve. It's not magic, and it's not one thing.

List Hygiene

Every account in the table above runs active suppression. Unengaged subscribers — people who haven't opened in 90–180 days, depending on send frequency — are run through a win-back sequence and then suppressed if they don't re-engage.

When you remove unengaged subscribers from your active send lists, your open rate rises because you're calculating the percentage against a smaller denominator of people who actually want your emails. This is not gaming the metric — it's the correct way to measure email engagement.

Segmentation

Sending the same email to everyone on your list is the fastest way to mediocre open rates. Every account above sends different content to different segments: buyers vs. non-buyers, product category interests, engagement recency, and purchase frequency. Someone who bought once in the past year gets a different email than someone on their sixth order.

Higher relevance drives higher open rates. This is not a surprising insight, but the number of brands that don't act on it is.

Subject Line Quality

Subject lines are the only variable that determines whether an email gets opened before someone sees a single word of your copy. An A/B test on subject lines in a well-segmented list with good deliverability can move open rates 15–20 points. Most brands run no subject line tests at all.

What works: specificity, curiosity without being vague, and copy that sounds like it was written by a person who knows the subscriber. What doesn't work: "Our biggest sale ever," "Don't miss out," and subject lines that give away everything before the subscriber opens.

Sender Reputation and Deliverability

An email with a 70% open rate means nothing if 30% of your sends are landing in spam. Sender reputation — driven by engagement rates, spam complaints, and bounce management — determines what percentage of your sends actually reach the inbox.

The accounts above maintain low complaint rates (under 0.1%) by sending relevant content to engaged lists. High spam complaints are usually a list hygiene problem in disguise: you're emailing people who don't want to hear from you, and they're marking your emails as spam instead of unsubscribing.

Campaign Open Rates vs. Flow Open Rates

A useful distinction that most benchmark reports collapse: campaigns and flows should be measured separately.

Flows — automated sequences triggered by subscriber behaviour — typically outperform campaigns on open rate because they're sent at moments of high relevance. An abandoned cart email arriving an hour after someone left your site is inherently more relevant than a weekly newsletter sent at a fixed time to a broad segment.

Across the accounts we manage, flow open rates run 5–15 points higher than campaign open rates in most cases. If yours are lower, there's a structural issue — either the flows are triggering incorrectly or the copy inside them doesn't match the behaviour that triggered them.

How to Benchmark Your Own Account

When you're assessing your own performance, compare against the right cohort:

  • Compare sends to engaged segments only, not your full list — total-list averages mix engaged and unengaged subscribers and give you a number that's neither accurate nor actionable
  • Separate campaigns from flows in your reporting — they serve different purposes and have different open rate baselines
  • Track 90-day trailing averages, not individual send performance — single-send open rates fluctuate based on subject line, time, and external events
  • Account for iOS MPP inflation if you're comparing recent numbers to pre-2021 benchmarks — Apple Mail Privacy Protection introduced machine opens that count as opens in Klaviyo's reporting

What a Realistic Target Looks Like

For a DTC brand with reasonable list hygiene and basic segmentation, a realistic campaign open rate target is 35–50%. Brands with well-optimised systems, clean lists, and strong copy push 50–80%.

If you're consistently below 30% on engaged segments, there's usually a deliverability or relevance problem. If you're above 80%, check whether MPP is inflating your numbers — genuine 80%+ open rates exist, but they require deliberate work to get there and maintain.

The number isn't the goal. Revenue is the goal. Open rate is an input metric — it tells you whether your emails are getting attention, which determines whether your copy ever gets a chance to convert. Fix open rates not because the number matters, but because higher open rates mean more people seeing your offers.
