POST-PURCHASE FLOWS:
ONE ORDER INTO
A REPEAT CUSTOMER.

Most brands go quiet the moment an order ships. That silence is a missed opportunity: the post-purchase window is where retention actually gets built.

UROS KORENE Founder, OpenRateLab

A customer who just bought is a different subscriber than one you're still trying to convert. They've already made the trust decision. The abandoned cart and welcome flows exist to get that first purchase. The post-purchase flow exists to make sure it isn't the last one.

Why Brands Skip This Flow

It's the most commonly under-built flow we see when auditing new accounts. Most Klaviyo setups have a welcome series and an abandoned cart flow, because those map directly to obvious revenue moments. Post-purchase gets deprioritized because its ROI is less immediate: it's about the second and third order, not the first, so it's easy to treat as optional.

That's a mistake. A customer who's already bought once is dramatically cheaper to sell to again than a new prospect, and the post-purchase window is when their trust in the brand is highest.

The 5-Part Post-Purchase Sequence

  • Order confirmation (immediate): Not just a transactional receipt. This is a real brand touchpoint: confirm the order clearly, but use actual voice, not boilerplate. It's often the highest open-rate email in the entire flow system because the customer is actively expecting it.
  • Shipping update (on fulfillment): Tracking info plus a reason to stay engaged: what to expect, care instructions, or a preview of what's coming. Skip the pure "your order has shipped" email with nothing else in it.
  • Product education (a few days after delivery): How to get the most out of what they bought. This reduces returns, increases satisfaction, and sets up the next email by making sure the customer actually used the product well.
  • Review request (1-3 weeks post-delivery): Timed to arrive after genuine use, not immediately after delivery. Reviews collected too early are based on unboxing, not experience, and read thinner as a result.
  • Cross-sell or replenishment (timed to your product's usage cycle): A consumable product needs this sooner than a durable one. This is where post-purchase starts generating a second order, not just goodwill.

Why Review Timing Matters

Asking for a review the day after delivery gets reviews about packaging and shipping speed, not the product itself. Waiting until the customer has genuinely used the item produces reviews that actually help convert future buyers, because they're specific: how it performed, how it fit, whether it lived up to the description. That specificity is what makes a review persuasive to the next shopper reading it.

Post-Purchase Is Preventive, Not Just Additive

A strong post-purchase flow doesn't just generate a second sale. It reduces how many customers ever need a win-back flow later, because fewer of them go quiet in the first place. Retention built early is cheaper than retention recovered late.

Setting the Trigger in Klaviyo

Trigger on "Placed Order" for the confirmation email, then branch subsequent emails off fulfillment and delivery events from your e-commerce platform integration (Shopify's "Fulfilled Order" event, for example). Time the education and review emails using delay steps calibrated to your actual shipping speed and typical usage timeline, not a fixed universal number.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

What should be in a post-purchase email flow? [+]

An order confirmation with real brand voice (not just a receipt), shipping updates, a product education or usage email so the customer gets full value from what they bought, and a review request timed to arrive after they've actually used the product. A cross-sell or replenishment email closes the sequence, timed to your product's real usage cycle.

When should you ask for a review after purchase? [+]

After the customer has had genuine time to use the product, not immediately after delivery. For most physical products, that's 1 to 3 weeks post-delivery. Asking too early gets reviews based on unboxing, not actual product experience, which produces thinner, less persuasive reviews.

How is post-purchase different from a win-back flow? [+]

Post-purchase starts immediately after an order and is about maximizing the value of that specific purchase and setting up the next one. Win-back is a much later intervention, triggered when someone hasn't engaged or purchased in 90+ days, aimed at recovering a relationship that's already gone cold. A strong post-purchase flow reduces how many customers ever need a win-back flow in the first place.
