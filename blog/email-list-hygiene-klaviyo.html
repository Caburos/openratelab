EMAIL LIST HYGIENE
IN KLAVIYO: HOW TO CLEAN
WITHOUT LOSING REVENUE.

Most brands either never clean their list (bad for deliverability) or suppress too aggressively (burns winnable subscribers). Here's the 4-tier segmentation system that protects both.

List hygiene is one of those email marketing topics that sounds like admin work but has a direct line to revenue. A dirty list doesn't just drag down your open rates — it damages your sender reputation, which means fewer of your emails reach the inbox across your entire subscriber base, including your best customers.

At the same time, suppressing too aggressively loses you subscribers who would have converted with one more well-timed email. The goal isn't to shrink your list. It's to send the right emails to the right people and stop sending to the ones who've genuinely disengaged.

Here's the system we use on active accounts.

Why List Hygiene Affects Deliverability

Email deliverability is determined in large part by how Gmail, Outlook, and other providers assess your sending reputation. They look at engagement signals: open rates, click rates, spam complaint rates, and whether people are unsubscribing or deleting without opening.

When you consistently send to people who don't open, providers register that your emails aren't wanted. Over time, they start routing your emails to the promotions tab, the spam folder, or simply not delivering them at all. This affects every email you send — including to your engaged subscribers who do want to hear from you.

The fix isn't sending fewer emails. It's sending to the right people.

The 4-Tier Engagement Segmentation

Rather than a binary "engaged / not engaged" split, we use a four-tier structure that lets you send different content at different frequencies to each group, and run a win-back before any suppression happens.

VIP
OPENED IN 30 DAYS
OR 2+ PURCHASES
Your most engaged subscribers and your best customers. Send everything to this segment — campaigns, flow emails, product launches. High frequency is appropriate here. These people want to hear from you.
ENGAGED
OPENED IN 60–90 DAYS
Active but not highly frequent openers. Send campaigns and major flows, but reduce promotional frequency. These subscribers are warming or cooling depending on what you send. Good content keeps them in this tier; spam pushes them to Lapsing.
LAPSING
NO OPEN IN 90–150 DAYS
Danger zone. These subscribers are disengaging. Send 1–2 campaigns per month maximum — only your highest-value content. Trigger a win-back flow at the 90-day mark. The goal is re-engagement or an informed decision to stop sending.
UNENGAGED
NO OPEN IN 150+ DAYS
Run a final win-back. If no engagement, suppress. Do not continue sending to this segment — you're paying for contacts that aren't opening and actively hurting your sender reputation with every send.

The thresholds above (30/60–90/90–150/150+) are starting points. Adjust based on your send frequency — a brand that sends daily needs shorter windows than one sending weekly. What matters is the structure: engagement tiers with different treatment at each level.

The Win-Back Flow: Before You Suppress

Suppression should always be preceded by an attempt to win back. Some lapsed subscribers genuinely just missed your emails — life got busy, they changed inboxes, or they went quiet and would re-engage with the right prompt.

A win-back flow for the Lapsing and Unengaged tiers looks like this:

  • Email 1 (day 0): Re-engagement offer or strong value proposition. Subject line: make it about them, not about your brand. "We noticed you haven't been around" is weak. "Here's what you've missed" is stronger. A compelling offer or genuinely useful piece of content is stronger still.
  • Email 2 (day 5): Final notice. Tell them you're going to stop emailing them unless they'd like to stay on the list. This transparency works — some people re-engage just because they were given the choice. It also filters out the people who genuinely want to leave.
  • Suppression (day 7 if no engagement): No third email. Suppress.

A two-email win-back that results in suppression is still a win. You've identified dead weight, protected your deliverability, and potentially recovered some subscribers who just needed the prompt.

How to Set This Up in Klaviyo

Step 1: Build the Four Segments

In Klaviyo, go to Segments and create four segments using the following conditions:

  • VIP: "Has opened email at least once in the last 30 days" OR "Has placed order at least 2 times"
  • Engaged: "Has opened email at least once in the last 90 days" AND NOT VIP
  • Lapsing: "Has opened email at least once in the last 150 days" AND NOT Engaged AND NOT VIP
  • Unengaged: "Is in List [your main list]" AND NOT Lapsing AND NOT Engaged AND NOT VIP — i.e., everyone who hasn't opened in 150+ days

Note: Klaviyo's open tracking is affected by iOS MPP, which inflates open counts on Apple Mail. If you're seeing artificially high "opened" signals, you may want to use click-based engagement as an alternative or supplementary signal.

Step 2: Create the Win-Back Flow

Build a flow triggered by "Joins Segment" — specifically, when someone enters your Lapsing segment. Set a delay of 2–3 days to avoid triggering on subscribers who only recently went quiet.

Inside the flow: Email 1 → 5-day delay → Email 2 → 7-day delay → Conditional split on "Has opened email since starting this flow" → suppress the "No" branch.

Step 3: Suppress the Unengaged Segment

Before suppressing at scale, run the win-back flow for 30–60 days to give lapsing subscribers the chance to re-engage. Then go to your Unengaged segment and suppress in bulk.

Klaviyo allows you to suppress profiles, which prevents them from receiving emails without deleting their data. Suppressed profiles can be unsuppressed if they re-subscribe — this is the correct approach over deletion.

How Often to Run This Process

This isn't a one-time cleanup. Build the segments once, set up the win-back flow, and let it run continuously. New subscribers will move through tiers based on their behaviour without any manual intervention.

Review the segment sizes quarterly. If your Lapsing segment is growing faster than your Engaged segment, you have a content or frequency problem — you're either sending too much or the content isn't compelling enough to maintain engagement.

What to Expect After Cleaning

The first measurable effect after suppressing unengaged subscribers is a drop in your total list size — which can feel counterintuitive. You're paying for fewer contacts in Klaviyo, and your "list size" metric looks smaller. Both of these are positives.

Over the following 30–60 days, you should see:

  • Open rate increase — you're now calculating opens against a smaller, more engaged denominator
  • Spam complaint rate drop — you've removed the segment most likely to be marking you as spam
  • Inbox placement improvement — Gmail and Outlook see more positive engagement signals per send and start routing your emails to the primary inbox more consistently
  • Stable or improved revenue — unengaged subscribers were contributing almost nothing to revenue; removing them rarely impacts the bottom line

What list hygiene doesn't fix: copy quality, offer relevance, or a broken flow architecture. If your engaged subscribers aren't converting, that's a different problem. List hygiene removes the noise so you can hear the signal clearly.

The Most Common Mistake

Brands either wait too long to suppress (and let deliverability decay for months) or suppress based on arbitrary rules without running a win-back first.

The two-email win-back before suppression is not optional. It recovers a meaningful percentage of subscribers who would otherwise be silently suppressed, and it gives you clean data: people who didn't re-engage despite a direct ask are genuinely disengaged. There's no revenue left on the table when you suppress them.

Do the win-back. Then suppress without guilt.
