HOW TO CHOOSE
A KLAVIYO AGENCY:
QUESTIONS THAT MATTER.

Every agency's homepage says they'll grow your revenue. Here's what actually separates the ones that do it from the ones that just send emails on schedule.

UROS KORENE Founder, OpenRateLab

We wrote this as a genuine evaluation guide, including the questions that would work against us if we didn't hold up under them. If you're comparing agencies, use this as a checklist regardless of who you end up choosing.

Ask for Real Numbers, Not Testimonials

A testimonial saying "they grew our revenue" tells you nothing verifiable. Ask instead: what open rate, click rate, and email-attributed revenue are you currently seeing on accounts like mine, and how is that revenue attributed? A serious agency can answer specifically, ideally with a case study you can read in full, not just a pull-quote.

Be skeptical of round, suspiciously clean numbers with no context. Real performance data has texture: it varies by industry, list size, and send frequency. "We doubled everyone's revenue" is marketing copy. "43.3% open rate and 2.86% click rate sustained across 50 campaigns on an activewear account" is a claim you can actually verify against a published case study.

Ask Who Actually Works on Your Account

The person on the sales call is not always the person who writes your emails or builds your flows. This is normal at larger agencies, but you should know it going in. Ask directly: who is doing the strategy, who is writing the copy, and how many other accounts does that person manage. An agency stretching one senior strategist across forty accounts is going to give you less attention than one running a focused roster.

Ask What Month One Actually Looks Like

Vague onboarding promises ("we'll get to know your brand") are a red flag. A real onboarding plan is specific: an audit of your current flows and list health in week one, a prioritized fix list in week two, and a build timeline for the highest-impact gaps. If an agency can't tell you what they'd do in the first 30 days without first being hired, that's worth noting.

  • Week 1: Full audit of existing flows, segmentation, and list health. This should surface specific, named issues, not generic advice.
  • Week 2–3: Prioritized roadmap based on what's actually broken or missing, ranked by expected revenue impact.
  • Week 4+: Build begins on the highest-priority items first, not a fixed 90-day plan applied identically to every account.

Pricing Structure Should Match Your Situation, Not Their Template

Pricing varies. Fixed monthly retainers, hourly rates, and performance-based arrangements each make sense in different situations, and a good agency should be able to explain why they're recommending one over the others for you specifically. Be cautious of any agency quoting a firm price before understanding your list size, current flow setup, and send volume: pricing before scoping usually means a generic package, not a plan built for your account.

Ask What's Wrong With Your Current Setup

This is the single best test. Before you sign anything, a competent agency should be able to look at your current Klaviyo account and tell you specifically what's underperforming: a missing flow, a segmentation gap, subject lines that aren't earning opens. If the answer is vague ("everything could be better"), that's not an audit, that's a sales pitch. A specific answer, even an uncomfortable one, tells you they're actually looking at your data.

Agency vs. In-House: Not Always an Either/Or

Some brands need a full-time in-house hire once email becomes their largest revenue channel and requires daily attention. Others need an agency's senior-level flow architecture and copywriting without adding headcount. Many run both: an agency handling strategy and flow builds, an in-house coordinator managing day-to-day sends. Neither is universally correct. It depends on volume, internal bandwidth, and whether you need ongoing strategic thinking or execution support.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

How much does a Klaviyo agency cost? [+]

It varies by scope, not a fixed rate card. Copy-only engagements cost less than full account management. Pricing structures also differ: fixed monthly retainers, hourly, or performance-based, depending on what fits the account. Be wary of any agency quoting a price before understanding your list size, send volume, and current flow setup. Scope should come before price.

Should I hire a Klaviyo agency or an in-house email marketer? [+]

It depends on volume and specialization needs. An in-house hire makes sense once email is consistently your largest revenue channel and needs daily attention. An agency makes sense when you need senior-level flow architecture and copywriting without a full-time headcount, or when you want a second set of eyes auditing an existing in-house setup. Many brands run both: an agency for strategy and flow builds, an in-house coordinator for day-to-day sends.

What questions should I ask a Klaviyo agency before hiring? [+]

Ask for real client data, not just testimonials: open rates, revenue attribution, and how those numbers are measured. Ask who will actually work on your account, not just who's on the sales call. Ask what happens in month one versus month three: a real onboarding plan sounds different from a vague promise. And ask what they'd flag as wrong with your current setup during a free audit, before you've signed anything. Their answer tells you how they actually think.
