THE KLAVIYO
WELCOME SERIES
THAT ACTUALLY CONVERTS.

A new subscriber is at peak interest the moment they sign up. Most brands waste that window on a single discount email. Here's the structure we use instead.

UROS KORENE Founder, OpenRateLab

The welcome series is one of the first entries in any account's core flow architecture, and for good reason: it reaches subscribers at the single highest-intent moment they'll ever have with your brand. They just signed up. They're curious. That attention decays fast if the first thing they get is a coupon and nothing else.

Why a Single Discount Email Isn't Enough

A one-email welcome sequence with just a discount code treats every new subscriber the same way: as a price-sensitive shopper who needs a nudge. Some subscribers are ready to buy immediately. Others need to understand what the brand actually is before they'll spend money. A single email can't do both jobs.

Leading purely on discount also has a longer-term cost: it trains subscribers to wait for the next deal instead of buying at full price, which compounds against margin over time.

Structuring a 4-Email Welcome Series

The exact count varies by account, but 3 to 5 emails is the range that consistently performs well. Here's a structure we use as a starting point:

  • Email 1 (immediate): Welcome and brand introduction. What the brand actually stands for, not a generic "thanks for signing up." If there's a signup incentive, include it here, but pair it with something real: the story, the differentiation, the reason to care beyond the percentage off.
  • Email 2 (day 1-2): Social proof or product education. Best-sellers, how the product works, or real customer results. This is where you build the case for someone who isn't ready to buy on day one.
  • Email 3 (day 3-4): Objection handling. Whatever's actually stopping people from buying, address it directly: shipping concerns, sizing, return policy, ingredient questions. Pull this from real customer service data if you have it.
  • Email 4 (day 5-7): Urgency or a final incentive. If the discount hasn't been used yet, this is where a reminder or expiration creates a reason to act now instead of later.

Exit the Flow the Moment Someone Converts

The welcome series needs a clean exit condition: first purchase, or a fixed number of days, whichever happens first. Someone who buys on email 2 should immediately exit into post-purchase flows, not keep receiving welcome content meant for someone who hasn't bought yet. In Klaviyo, this is a straightforward flow filter: exclude anyone who's placed an order since entering the flow.

Setting the Trigger

Trigger this flow on "Subscribed to List" or "Consented to Email Marketing" depending on how your signup forms are configured. Avoid triggering on "Joins Segment" for a general newsletter segment: that can fire for people who were added in bulk (a CSV import, for example) rather than someone actively opting in, which sends a welcome sequence to people who never asked for one.

Common Welcome Series Mistakes

The two we see most often: sending the entire series at the same cadence regardless of engagement, and never testing whether the discount is actually necessary. Some brands find their welcome series converts just as well without a discount once the copy is doing real work; others depend on it heavily. That's worth testing rather than assuming.

The other mistake is treating the welcome series as "set and forget." It should be reviewed alongside open rate benchmarks quarterly, the same as any other flow, since it's usually one of the highest-volume flows in the account by subscriber count.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

How many emails should a Klaviyo welcome series have? [+]

3 to 5 emails is the range we build on most accounts. Fewer than 3 usually wastes the highest-intent window by rushing to a single discount ask. More than 5 risks fatigue before the subscriber has even made a first purchase. The right number depends on how much story the brand has to tell and how strong the product is on its own.

Should the first welcome email include a discount? [+]

Not always, and rarely as the only content. If a signup discount is core to the acquisition strategy, it should still be paired with something that builds trust: the brand's actual differentiation, not just a percentage off. Leading with pure discount trains subscribers to wait for deals instead of buying at full price, which erodes margin over time.

When does the welcome series end and the regular flow start? [+]

The welcome series should end with a clear exit condition, usually first purchase or a fixed number of days, whichever comes first. Once someone converts, they should exit into post-purchase flows, not keep receiving welcome content. Someone who doesn't convert within the window moves into regular campaign sends at a normal cadence, not an indefinite welcome loop.
