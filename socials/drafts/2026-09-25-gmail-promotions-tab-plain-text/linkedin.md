A welcome email kept landing in Gmail's Promotions tab. Five times. The plain-text version of the same message hit Primary on the first try.

Gmail's classifier isn't reading your copy for warmth. It's reading formatting and engagement signals: heavy HTML structure, image-to-text ratio, marketing-style layout patterns, and how that recipient has engaged with your domain before.

Welcome emails are especially exposed. They're usually the most heavily templated, most on-brand email in the whole flow, full header image, brand colors, multiple blocks. That's exactly the shape Gmail's classifier is tuned to catch.

This doesn't mean send everything as plain text. Promotions isn't a penalty box, and most marketing content belongs there. The real question is which specific emails, welcome, receipts, key lifecycle moments, actually need Primary placement.

Test it directly: send to a Gmail account you control and check the actual tab, don't guess from open rate.
