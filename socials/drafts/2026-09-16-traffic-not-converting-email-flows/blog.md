Traffic but no conversions gets diagnosed as an ads problem or a landing page problem almost every time. Check your email flows first. It's the fastest, cheapest thing to rule out, and it's often the actual leak.

## Why This Gets Misdiagnosed First

When traffic isn't converting, the instinct is to look at what the visitor sees: the ad that brought them, the landing page they landed on, the offer on the page. All of that matters eventually. None of it explains what happens to the visitor who leaves without converting and never hears from the brand again.

That's the gap flows exist to close, and it's the one most diagnostics skip past on the way to testing a new landing page variant.

## The Three Flows That Catch Traffic You Already Have

A welcome flow, an abandoned cart flow, and browse abandonment capture are the three mechanisms that turn a single visit into a second touch. Each one catches traffic at a different point: welcome catches new subscribers before they forget why they signed up, abandoned cart catches people who got close to buying, browse abandonment catches people who showed real interest without adding anything to cart.

If any one of these is missing, weak, or slow, that's a specific, identifiable leak; not a vague "conversion problem" that needs a broader fix.

## How To Check Fast

Start with whether a welcome flow exists and how quickly it fires. It should send within minutes of signup, not hours or days; by the time a day passes, most of the intent that made someone subscribe has already faded.

Check whether an abandoned cart flow exists and starts within roughly an hour of cart abandonment. Waiting longer means competing against whatever else the visitor found in the meantime. Our guide to [abandoned cart flow structure and timing](/blog/klaviyo-abandoned-cart-flow) covers the specific timing windows that actually recover carts.

Check for any browse abandonment capture at all. Many stores have zero mechanism for this, which means every visitor who looked at a product and left without adding to cart gets no follow-up whatsoever, even though they showed real, specific interest.

## Why This Comes Before Landing Page Work, Not After

A landing page fix only helps the visitors who are still on the page when you make the fix. Weak or missing flows mean every visitor who leaves without converting also leaves without a follow-up, which is a much larger group than whoever happens to be on the page at any given moment.

Fixing the landing page without fixing that leak means optimizing a smaller slice of the actual traffic. Once flows are solid and actually catching people who leave, landing page and CRO work has more traffic left over to work with, and the returns on that work go up.

Our [Klaviyo welcome series guide](/blog/klaviyo-welcome-series-guide) covers what a welcome flow needs to actually convert new subscribers instead of just confirming a signup.

## What "Good Enough" Looks Like Before Moving On

At a baseline level, before investing in deeper optimization: a welcome flow live and firing within minutes, an abandoned cart flow live and firing within about an hour, and some browse abandonment mechanism in place, even a simple one. That's the floor, not the ceiling. Once all three exist and are firing on time, the traffic-but-no-conversion diagnosis moves on to genuinely different causes, like offer, price, or landing page itself, with much more confidence that the flows layer isn't quietly eating the numbers underneath.
